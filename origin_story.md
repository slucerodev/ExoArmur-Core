# ExoArmur Origin Story
# Shea Lucero - shea83409@gmail.com

## The Short Version

I built a production-grade deterministic AI governance runtime in 14 months using nothing but natural language and AI coding assistants. I'm not an engineer. I have no formal training in systems development. I was just a guy on a couch with a laptop and curiosity.

## The Full Story

I work odd jobs—mostly as a contract repairman for houses being flipped or rented out. There's not really a title for what I do, but it gives me a flexible schedule. About 14 months ago, LLMs started dominating the news. Every week, another big company was releasing more advanced, more capable models. The race was on.

I got extremely interested. I started wondering: what are the limits of these things? From my couch, I hopped on my laptop and began learning what they were and what they could do.

Then I started wondering a different question: how would these systems be "governed" or kept in check to prevent the runaway AI scenarios you see in movies? I didn't even know "AI governance" was a field—I just used the term coincidentally.

I searched for existing solutions and didn't find much. So I wondered: could the very tools I was asking about governing be capable of building their own governance with my natural language guidance?

This was right around the time Andrej Karpathy coined the term "vibe coding"—natural language development where you guide AI to write code. That's exactly how ExoArmur was created, designed, and engineered.

Over 14 months, I used WindSurf IDE along with multiple LLMs to architect and build what became ExoArmur. It went through several iterations. At one point, I scrapped 6 months of work and started completely over.

I am not an engineer. I'm not in the industry. I have no formal or former training in systems engineering or development. I was simply a guy with a laptop and curiosity, willing to learn.

## What Emerged

The system I ended up with has:
- **1,041 passing tests** with a three-run stability gate
- **Deterministic replay verification** (same inputs = identical outputs)
- **Cryptographic audit trails** that can't be tampered with
- **Single execution boundary** enforcing all governance controls
- **Federation layer** for multi-cell coordination
- **Byzantine fault tolerance** components

It's published on PyPI. It has comprehensive documentation. It's production-ready.
